## The belief
"Random means anything can happen": with five dice, a sum above 25 is as likely as a sum in the middle.
Picture the belief predicts: 26 sums [nSums] from 5 to 30 [sumMin, sumMax], all equally likely, about 3,846
rolls each [beliefEach], so 5 of 26 sums above 25 would hold 19,231 rolls [beliefTail] (about 20,000
[beliefGuess]).

## The gap (the reversal)
- Belief: a flat field of sums. Count: a bell. One die is flat (each first-die pile between 16,519 and 16,797
  [firstMin, firstMax] of 100,000 [rolls]); the sum of five is not: the middle sums 17 and 18 [modeLo, modeHi]
  each take 10.0 % [pModePct], the end sums 0.013 % [pEdgePct].
- The tail above 25 holds 1,655 rolls [simTail], not 19,231: the belief over-counts it about 12 times
  [beliefTimes]. The exact count of ways agrees: 126 of 7,776 [waysTail, outcomes] → 1,620 expected
  [expTailRound], 1.6 % [tailPctExact]; the dice gave 1.6 % [tailPctSim], 35 more rolls [tailGap].

## The mechanism (what THE COUNT draws)
A sum is a vote of five dice. There is one way to make 5 (all ones) and one way to make 30 (all sixes)
[ways5, ways30], but 780 ways to make 17 and 780 to make 18 [ways17, ways18]: the middle is crowded because
highs and lows cancel in many more arrangements than they align. Counting the 7,776 equally likely outcomes
[outcomes] by inclusion-exclusion, ways(s) = Σₖ (−1)ᵏ C(5,k) C(s−6k−1, 4), gives the bell exactly; the
simulation gives it again by brute force. The spread is fixed by arithmetic, not luck: mean 17.5 [meanExact]
and standard deviation √(5·35/12) = 3.82 [sdExact]; the 100,000 rolls give 17.50 and 3.81 [simMean, simSd].
Even the first die cannot break it alone: when it shows 1 no sum can pass 25 [tailFirst1 = 0]; when it shows 6,
926 of its rolls do [tailFirst6]. Across all 26 sums, counted frequency and exact probability differ by at most
0.09 points [maxGapPts], at sum 14 [maxGapSum] (87 rolls [maxGapRolls]).

## The fixture
One seeded stream, reproducible to the bit: 100,000 rolls [rolls] of five fair dice [dice], six faces [faces],
generated by mulberry32 [S4] with seed 1733 [seed] in this package (2026) [S5], checked against the exact
distribution over 7,776 outcomes [S3]. It is a canonical worked example, not a historical dataset; the exact
combinatorics carry the truth and the simulation is the count the viewer watches.

**The generator (bit-exact contract for the drafters; recompute.py mirrors it in uint32 arithmetic):**
```js
function mulberry32(a) { return function () { a |= 0; a = a + 0x6D2B79F5 | 0;
  let t = Math.imul(a ^ a >>> 15, 1 | a); t = t + Math.imul(t ^ t >>> 7, 61 | t) ^ t;
  return ((t ^ t >>> 14) >>> 0) / 4294967296; }; }
const rnd = mulberry32(1733);                        // ONE stream for the whole film
for (let i = 0; i < 100000; i++)                     // roll i = arrival order; count(t) = k shows rolls 0..k-1
  for (let j = 0; j < 5; j++)                        // die j; j = 0 is "the first die"
    face[i][j] = 1 + Math.floor(rnd() * 6);          // draw number 5*i + j
sum[i] = face[i][0] + face[i][1] + face[i][2] + face[i][3] + face[i][4];
```
Roll 0 is 2-3-2-5-6, sum 18 [roll0Sum]. `data/checkpoints.json` lists the first 10 rolls and the tail /
first-die-6 / sum-of-sums tallies after 1, 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000, 50,000 and 100,000 rolls: a draft's
`count(t)` readouts must match them at those k (an in-film assertion, not a picture).

## Monday
Question: "Is the total you are planning a sum of many small, separate parts? Then plan for the middle."
Honest limit (one line): fair dice rolled independently; parts that move together, or one part that dwarfs
the rest, do not make this bell.

## NotThis
- Not a normal-curve formula lesson: no Gaussian density is drawn; the bell over the cut is the exact count
  of ways, a step outline over 26 integers, not a smooth curve (the CLT is the limit; five dice are not it).
- Not "the law of averages" (that the dice owe you a 30): the film never says rolls compensate.
- Not Weldon's twelve-dice throws (reported by Pearson, 1900): a real historic dataset, but its tables
  would need their own claims and twelve dice change every number; a later film.
- Not a 3D picture for its own sake: the camera move is the claim (from above every sum looks alike; from the
  side it does not), the volume's cut is the count, and the 100,000 marks are beyond Canvas2D at true scale.
- Not a commit film (D11): no sealed guess, no countdown.
