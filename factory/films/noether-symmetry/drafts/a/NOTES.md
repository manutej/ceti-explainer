# noether-symmetry · draft A · "the observatory"

Register: a dark sky (faint stars, dotted ecliptic rings). Earth's two measured moments are two lit dots on an ellipse with r as a thin arm and v as an
arrow (both drawn to scale: the real orbit is almost a circle, so the picture says so), and the formula "distance × speed" binds into both, twice.
Then 3,000 simulated orbit states hang as a cloud the camera orbits slowly; on the big move (60–68 s) every dot travels its own straight path to the tip of
its orbit's spin arrow (300 specks on six thin shells, colour = energy), a translucent plane cuts the shells at the equator (62 orbits, 620 dots lit), and
the shells regroup by energy into five layers while the camera cranes down to look at them edge-on (88–96 s). The same move for the 3,000 training states of
a tiny network (112–119 s), a wordless smoke-ring plate (131.5–135 s), a wide frame with both shell pictures side by side, and the honest line on stage.
Look: brand ceti-coastal-dark, chrome none, material ink, level manager, renderer webgl, format feature-long, dur 153, commit off (D11).
Chapters HOOK 0–12 · CASE 12–60 · COUNT 60–135 · MONDAY 135–150 · card 150–153. count.at 26.0 (the 3,000 lands; the first ratio is 0.011 % at 53.5 s).

## Chain (and what was changed)
- gl-pointcloud: **patched copy** (lib/gl-pointcloud.js, header says what). Added: per-point HOME coordinates (aPosition) and two more places (aTo spin space,
  aTo2 layers) as vertex properties plus a per-point delay; `m1`, `m2` in [0,1] move each point along its own straight path home → aTo → aTo2, pure of t
  (the film computes them from t; `at()` is the CPU twin for pins). Also a colour table by level, a count-in order (`shown`), a feature rank (`f`: lights one
  orbit or run one dot at a time, `fdim` sinks the rest), a cut flag (`cut`: dims the rest, never removes), a ring mode (the smoke ring: positions from t in the
  shader). Removed: DoF, HUD, pins, axes, synthetic data, the module camera. Same marks, same count: no dot is added, removed or faded in a move.
- gl-camera-rig: verbatim, demo scene cut by assemble.py. The whole camera is one script (lib/cam.base.json, written by lib/mkfilm.py); every move/key is a knob
  through `toKnobs` (71 camera knobs; ease rows dropped to save bytes). The film's morph windows read the rig's move times, so one knob moves both.
- gl-labels: verbatim `solve` and `project` (drawGL, kit and the demo scene cut). Every pin is solved on the rig's own camera, `sticky: window`, `reserve` = caption
  band, the tag, the formula zone and Earth's dots; drawn as kit SVG with a data-role.
- formula-bind: **not used as a module.** Its product form multiplies two integer counts into cells and draws its digits on canvas (not claims, G5c); r and v are
  measured decimals. Its grammar (set → bind → fly → compute) is written in film.src.js as SVG text: terms keep a role colour, words fly (one at a time, never
  crossing) to the marks they name, the numbers fly back into the slots, the product counts up. Run twice (closest, farthest), side by side.

## Data
lib/data.js (generated by lib/mkdata.py from factory/topics/noether-symmetry/data): the 300 orbits as spin direction + in-plane angle + phase at 12 bits (3.0 KB),
the 100 runs as 30 weight-space points each (1/100, delta coded, 9.3 KB) and the run's fixed triple (1/1000). The film rebuilds the 3,000 states with the Kepler
solution (Newton, 60 iterations) and never trains anything. Checked against data/orbits.json and data/network.json (scratch script, see below).
Choices: the shells are spheres of equal |L| in the spin-arrow space (F3), colour = energy level; the layers are the (E, |L|) picture (height = energy, radius =
|L|, angle = where the spin points), so "(E, L) space" is the five-layer end state and the shells are the move before it.

## Warnings left, written down
- G5c (WARN only): the product counts up (intermediate digits), and the pin "ORBITS LIT · 620 OF 3,000 DOTS" is read as "20" by the gate's render-stripping
  (claims.json renders '6' and '5' are removed first from every number, so a caption with 62, 620 or 1869 fails G5b: captions c23 and c36 avoid those digits; the
  62, 620 and 1869 figures live in pins and the set type, where only G5c reads them).
- G6 phone 390: horizontal overflow reported by the shell (live page), not by the film.
- The Kelvin year (1869) is not on screen: the plate is wordless and the caption avoids the digits (G5b artefact above).
- Earth's orbit is drawn to scale, so the ellipse is nearly a circle and the arm lengths differ by 3.4 % (a drawn difference you can barely see); the numbers carry it.
- The weight-space picture is a 3-D PCA of 48 weights (18.5 % of the variance); the film says "a picture of the weights" in no label and reads no axis.
- Stars, dotted rings and shell wires are guides, not data; they are drawn with the cloud shader (dots), not lines.
