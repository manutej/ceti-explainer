# Dissertation Direction — an operadic interview instrument

*Version 1.0 · respondent: Wen · answer type: ranked choice of {a coral microbiome, b deep-sea vents, c fisheries acoustics} with a dated ≤12-month kill-criterion on the #1 topic · depth budget 3*

---

## How to answer this

Answer any node at any depth, in any order, and **skip freely** — a blank slot is data too.

- A set of **deep (child) answers** composes upward into the parent's answer via the stated rule — that is *evidence-built*.
- A **direct answer at a shallow node** is its own *collapsed valuation* — your gut read, taken as-is.
- Answering **both** a parent directly *and* its children is not redundant: the gap between your collapsed answer and the composed one is the **built-in consistency check** — and that gap is exactly what a committee meeting exists to resolve.

Two facts are **already settled** and are not asked again — they are shown as valuations inside the tree:
- Your advisor's grant fully funds **(a) coral microbiome** only through **year 3**.
- **(c) fisheries acoustics** carries a **guaranteed industry stipend**, but the partner company **owns first publication review**.

If you answer nothing else, answer the **★ questions** — that starred set is the minimum viable interview.

No top-level question (Q1–Q5) carries a ★ on purpose: because Q0 composes its children as a **product** (`argmax over Q1 × Q2 × Q3 × Q4`), no single factor can substitute for the root — a near-zero on any one factor (unfundable, career-poisoning, unsustainable) zeroes the whole ranking. The minimum viable interview is therefore *delegated* to one revealed-behavior probe per factor (Q1.1, Q2.2, Q3.1, Q4.1) plus the kill-signal (Q5.1), not concentrated in any one top-level question.

---

**Q0 — Of the three directions — (a) coral microbiome resilience, (b) deep-sea vent chemosynthesis, (c) fisheries acoustics with the industry partner — which do you commit to first, how do you rank the other two behind it, and what single thing could you observe in the next 12 months that would make you drop the one you ranked first?**  `() → ranked choice + dated kill-criterion`
▷

> **Compose (root):** ranking = argmax over ( Q1 work-fit × Q2 funding-security × Q3 career-trajectory × Q4 life-sustainability ), with the revealed-behavior child double-weighted inside each factor; the three topics ordered by composed score. Kill-criterion = the single ≤12-month, hardest-to-rationalize observable signal from Q5, bound to the #1 topic. If a direct gut ranking at Q0 disagrees with the composed ranking, that disagreement is the item to take to committee.

---

- **Q1 — Which of the three topics' actual weekly work — the cruises, the assays, the bench hours, the R scripts — would you still want to be doing in month 30, and which would you dread?**  `family → work-fit ranking`
  ▷
  - **Q1.1 ★ — Think back to your most recent stretch of pure bench work — assay days with no fieldwork, pipetting from morning on. Walk through one such day hour by hour: when did the time start to drag?**
    ▷
  - **Q1.2 — Between a two-week cruise collecting samples and a two-week run of solo R analysis at your desk, which one do you protect on your calendar and which do you let slip when you're tired?**
    ▷
  - **Q1.3 — Which topic's core method have you never actually done yourself yet, and what could you do within one term to find out whether you even like it?**
    ▷
  - **Q1.4 — If every assay were run for you by someone else, for free, which topic suddenly becomes the one you'd pick — and what does that swap tell you about the bench work?**
    ▷

  > **Compose Q1:** weighted read, honesty-doubled — the lived episode (Q1.1) and the revealed-calendar contrast (Q1.2) count double against the aspiration answer (Q1.4). Rank topics by which one's real weekly work you'd defend at month 30.

- **Q2 — Given that (a)'s grant money runs out after year 3 and (c) comes with a guaranteed stipend, which topic can you actually pay for in years 4 and 5 without a scramble?**  `family → funding-security ranking`
  ▷
  - **Q2.1 — What is the funded runway of each topic?**  *(valued from evidence — not re-asked)*
    ▷ (a) advisor's grant covers through **year 3 only**; (c) **guaranteed industry stipend**; (b) **unstated — must be sourced by you.**
  - **Q2.2 ★ — The last time funding uncertainty actually touched your work — a delayed reimbursement, a cruise nearly cut, a line item denied — what happened, and how close did it get to derailing you?**
    ▷
  - **Q2.3 — If the year-3 money for (a) evaporated, what is your named backup source, and have you ever personally secured money like it before?**
    ▷
  - **Q2.4 — What would funding a deep-sea vent program (b) actually require — whose ship time, whose grant — and do you have any concrete evidence that door is open to you?**
    ▷

  > **Compose Q2:** filter-chain (∧) then rank — a topic you cannot fund past year 3 without a *named, previously-secured* backup is filtered down; survivors ranked by runway certainty.

- **Q3 — Which topic sets up the postdoc and publication record you want — and does the industry deal on (c) help you there or quietly cost you?**  `family → career-trajectory ranking`
  ▷
  - **Q3.1 ★ — The (c) partner owns first review of your papers: what does that clause actually let them do — delay, redact, veto — and have you or anyone you know published under an industry review gate, and what did it cost in practice?**  *(consequence of an evidence-settled fact — the fact is not re-asked, only its lived effect)*
    ▷
  - **Q3.2 — Picture the job talk that lands you a postdoc: which topic's results are on the slides, and who in your field would notice that work within a year of it publishing?**
    ▷
  - **Q3.3 — Which topic produces papers you're first author on and free to submit anywhere, versus papers gated by someone else's calendar or sign-off?**
    ▷
  - **Q3.4 — Which of the three has a literature that has surprised you lately — moving faster or slower than you expected — and what does that do to your timing to first paper?**
    ▷

  > **Compose Q3:** weighted read, honesty-doubled on the concrete job-talk / social-witness answer (Q3.2); publication freedom (Q3.1, Q3.3) applied as a hard discount against any gated topic.

- **Q4 — Which topic's real calendar — above all the long cruises — can your life in the university city and your relationship actually carry for four more years?**  `family → life-sustainability ranking`
  ▷
  - **Q4.1 ★ — Recall the longest research cruise you've done. Step through the days away: what did the distance cost at home, and what did your partner actually say when you got back?**
    ▷
  - **Q4.2 — How many weeks per year at sea does each topic demand, and where is the line — from experience — past which you know the relationship strains?**
    ▷
  - **Q4.3 — If sea time were unlimited and cost nothing at home, would your ranking change, and what does the gap between that answer and your real one tell you?**
    ▷
  - **Q4.4 — What would have to be true at home for the most cruise-heavy topic (b, deep-sea vents) to be survivable, and is that arrangement realistic within the next year?**
    ▷

  > **Compose Q4:** weighted read, honesty-doubled on the lived episode (Q4.1) over the aspiration answer (Q4.3); sea-weeks-per-topic (Q4.2) is the ordering factor.

- **Q5 — For whatever topic you rank first, what specific thing could you observe in the next 12 months that would tell you it's the wrong bet — early enough to still switch?**  `chosen topic → dated kill-signal`
  ▷
  - **Q5.1 ★ — Name one single observation — a failed pilot assay, a denied ship berth, a null result at sea, a funding letter that doesn't come — that, if it happened by month 12, would make you switch off your top topic. Name the observation and the exact deadline.**
    ▷
  - **Q5.2 — Which warning sign in a past project did you spot early but talk yourself out of, and how would you make sure you don't rationalize that same sign away this time?**
    ▷
  - **Q5.3 — Which failure signals for your top topic show up inside the first 12 months versus only in year 3 or 4, and which of the early ones is closest to a clean go/no-go test?**
    ▷
  - **Q5.4 — Who — on your committee or among your collaborators — would tell you honestly that a topic is failing before you'd admit it yourself, and by when would they say so?**
    ▷

  > **Compose Q5:** pool-then-rank — Q5.1 and Q5.3 build the pool of candidate kill-signals, filtered to those observable within 12 months (a kill-criterion that only fires in year 3 is useless here); Q5.2 and Q5.4 rank the pool by how hard each signal is to talk yourself out of. The top-ranked ≤12-month signal becomes the stated kill-criterion on your #1 topic.

---

*What happens next: fill the slots you can → child answers compose upward into each factor, and the four factors compose into a ranking with a bound kill-criterion → where a direct answer and its composed answer disagree, those disagreements are surfaced → you resolve them, and the ranked choice with its kill-criterion is the decision.*
