## 3. Audiences and level caps

- **Audiences** (map §3, L2 §1–4): Executive, Manager, Non-technical, Technical. **Ladder:** Glance (~30 s), Grasp (~2 min), Wield (learner acts), Master (explorable).
- **Caps per film** (map §4, [inf], excluding HYG): Glance ≤2, Grasp ≤4, Wield ≤3 plus one priming, Master ≤5. At most one each of INV, PF, REF, NAR; SE ≤2; PCR ≤2; RET ≤1 per 40 s.
- **Broken by the map's own §3 matrix:** three Glance cells hold three structures (Exec, Manager, Non-tech). Non-tech Wield holds four. Manager Grasp puts TW before ENS, and Non-tech Wield has TW with no ENS or NF upstream (P7); it also puts INV before TW (P12).

**Precedence (map §4; sources):**
- P1: PCR before its quantity's reveal (L1-F; CRIT-d9).
- P2: ENS ▷ NF ▷ percentage (L1-K; L3§3).
- P3: CF concrete ▷ morph ▷ schematic ▷ symbol (L1-C).
- P4: WE FULL ▷ GAP1 ▷ TWO-GAPS ▷ INV/RET (L1-B).
- P5: CC cases ▷ ALIGN ▷ NOTICE ▷ NAME; ANA SRC2 ▷ EXTRACT ▷ BREAK (L1-D, L1-H).
- P6: REPLACE follows MYTH in the same film (L1-G).
- P7: TW needs upstream ENS or NF on the same seed; COST as a mark (CRIT§1).
- P8: RET and SE follow the content they test, ≥1 beat gap, feedback mandatory (L1-I, L1-J).
- P9: CAL requires PCR; TALLY after REVEAL (L4§).
- P10: TRF last; FAR never cued (L4-I11).
- P11: PF only at Wield or above, before any EXPLAIN (L1-E).
- P12: INV after PCR and TW [inf].
- Open conflicts: PF vs WE, CC vs ANA, NAR vs REF, MER vs CF.

## 5. Lanes PED-L1 to PED-L4: coverage and top findings

**PED-L1, structures (A–M):**
1. Animation beats static by g=.23 (61 studies); most wins are confounds (Berney & Bétrancourt 2016; Tversky et al. 2002). Contested vs d=.37.
2. Video as addition g=.80; as replacement g=.28 (Noetel et al. 2021).
3. Expertise reversal (Kalyuga et al. 2003), via element interactivity (Chen et al. 2017).
4. Mayer medians inflated; independent signalling d=.38 (Alpizar 2020). Contested.
5. Worked examples g=.48, maths only (Barbieri 2023); prompts added to examples null.
6. Contrasting cases d=.50 over 336 tests; similarities-then-principle d=1.18 (Alfieri 2013).
7. PF conceptual g=.36, procedural −.03 (Sinha & Kapur 2021). Contested; L4 finds g=.22.
8. Refutation texts g=.41, 44 comparisons (Schroeder & Kucera 2022).
9. Testing g≈.5–.67 (Greving & Richter 2018 review); generation effect size not retrieved.
10. Self-explanation g=.55, 64 studies (Bisra et al. 2018); natural frequencies, executives n.s.

**PED-L2, audiences:**
1. Interaction drives learning; animation d=.37, 26 studies, decorative excluded (Höffler & Leutner 2007). Watch-only PhET students rarely generated ideas (Adams et al.) [E].
2. Expertise reversal d≈.45 to 2.99, often partial (Kalyuga 2007). Too wide to plan on.
3. Decorative "fun" distracted PhET learners [E].
4. Performance mode and illusion of explanatory depth (Rozenblit & Keil 2002) [E].
5. Productive-failure magnitude unverified, by L2's own statement.
6. Algorithm aversion after the same error (Dietvorst et al. 2015): pair errors with a visible catch.
7. Top chatbot misconception: expecting URL opening; 500 logs (arXiv 2510.25662); moderate reliability.
8. First-person hedges ("I'm not sure, but...") lowered over-reliance and improved accuracy; general hedges did not (Kim et al. 2024, n=404).
9. Ten sampled responses changed no trust (n=19, underpowered; arXiv 2503.16114).
10. Executive, manager and cross-cultural rules are thin: Dixit & Jain 1985 (old working paper); Gomez del Rey et al. 2016.

**PED-L3, animation and simulation (20 rules):**
1. Most animation wins are confounds: extra information, interactivity, prediction (Tversky et al. 2002).
2. Höffler & Leutner 2007: d=.37, 76 comparisons; representational .40, decorational −.05, procedural-motor 1.06.
3. Transience: segmenting helps novices, little for high prior knowledge (Boucheix & Guignard 2005; Spanjers et al. 2010). Persistent traces [inf].
4. Relational cues ≈66% and 61% vs entity cues ≈45% and 38% (Boucheix & Lowe, N=84); one animation, colour-confounded.
5. Physical-force experience raised quiz scores (Kontra et al. 2015); mouse-dragging not shown to do the same.
6. PhET implicit scaffolding: design research and one think-aloud only (Podolefsky et al.).
7. Animated transitions help tracking; stages ≈1 s; heavy choreography adds error (Heer & Robertson 2007, N=24).
8. HOPs beat intervals for 2–3 quantities (Hullman 2015; Kale 2018). Frame duration unspecified, so untested.
9. 50 quantile dots ≈97% of optimal decisions; CDFs nearly as good (Kay et al. 2016).
10. Natural frequencies 42% vs 5%; visuals lift the format 26%→51% (Binder et al. 2015). Sonification "mixed and mostly preliminary"; creator principles are not evidence.

**PED-L4, evidence of understanding (Wield and Master):**
1. Practice tests beat restudy (Adesope et al. 2017; g unverified). Interpolated tests halved mind-wandering from ~40% (Szpunar et al. 2013).
2. Wrong pre-guesses raised later recall: 75% vs 56% immediate; 55% vs 45% at one week (Richland et al. 2009). One factual passage.
3. PF separate meta g=.22 (CI −.06 to .51), ≈.6 after bias correction. Contested.
4. Hypercorrection .85 vs .76, young adults only (Eich et al. 2012); Fazio & Marsh γ≈.13 (n=46). No evidence a calibration display helps.
5. Doubling-time framing cut compounding underestimation 90%→67% (Schonger & Sele 2020, N=459). Direction of the 0.95^k error unknown [inf].
6. ICAP: passive < active < constructive < interactive (Chi & Wylie 2014). Shallow items miss the gap.
7. Seductive details harm through diversion; a relevance prompt removed the harm (Springer 2023, verified).
8. Near/far transfer taxonomy (Barnett & Ceci): recalled, unverified.
9. Stealth assessment (Shute & Rahimi) workable at small scale; no strong analytics source found.
10. Gamification: cognitive g=.49, 38 papers (Sailer & Homner 2020); rigorous designs keep only cognitive (g=.42, k=9). Badges and leaderboards unsupported.

**Contested or [inf] across lanes:** contested: animation magnitude, PF, Mayer signalling, hypercorrection, gamification beyond cognitive. [inf]: persistent traces, counterfactual animation, HOP frame timing, WE/CC/SE holds, LINGER, return intervals, cap numbers, TRF tolerance.
