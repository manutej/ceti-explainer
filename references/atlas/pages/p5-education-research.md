# p5.js education research

## Definition
p5.js education research is the small body of studies on teaching with p5 — editor features, creativity, storytelling — whose results are qualitative or small-sample; none compares p5 explainers with video or text [S204][S205][S206].

## Details
- CHI '23 (McNutt, Outkine, Chugh) studied four editions of an intro p5 course: linting and auto-formatting were well received, auto-refresh was polarizing (3.1 → 3.7 with delay and lint-aware refresh), and autocomplete was used in ~12% of sessions [S204].
- Students were wary of features that made learning feel too easy or cluttered the editor [S204].
- A KU Leuven study (39 students, one day) reported p5's documentation and fast feedback made it easy after a two-hour intro; interdisciplinary pairs scored slightly higher on socio-interactive creativity but lower on end products [S205].
- A Waterloo/Manitoba course (49 students) had students build p5 interactive stories; individual assignments rated 4.6 vs 3.4 of 7 for collaborative ones [S206].
- Sarachan's SIGCSE '19 talk proposed teaching non-majors p5 via interactive art on social issues; it does not mention journalism [S207].
- Shape Toolbox is a research editor extension for bidirectional Bézier editing [S352].
- Grades 3–6 clubs found nearly a quarter of students preferred p5 over child-friendly tools [S208].

## In explainer work
- The evidence supports editor ergonomics (lint, format, controlled refresh) more than any claim that interactive p5 explainers out-teach video; see [[open-questions]] [S204].
